## The client's need

A system in production listens to a conversation, transcribes it with Amazon Transcribe and has an LLM on Amazon Bedrock write a structured note. I was asked to evaluate replacing either or both with open-source models, without changing the interface the rest of the product uses.

## What I proposed

A decision memo with a cost model, a risk analysis and one clear recommendation: **migrate the transcription, not the LLM.**

Finalists were compared with sources and dates: Voxtral Transcribe 2 and Whisper large-v3 for speech, and Qwen3, gpt-oss-120b, Mistral and OpenAI models against the Bedrock baseline.

## The hard call

> I refused to draw the architecture before measuring.

Two unknowns, real volume and average duration, move the break-even anywhere between 54 and 217 conversations a day. An unconfirmed data-residency requirement could rule out every third-party option. So phase 0 is pulling real numbers from Cost Explorer, not a diagram.

I also named the risk that kills the easy answer. Whisper has a documented failure mode, inventing text during silence, that the current service does not. In a written record that is a false statement. The migration is justified by cost, but **only approved if a pilot shows the significant-error rate does not get worse.** And the baseline had its own problem: it used a locale that supports neither custom language models nor PII redaction, while a neighbouring locale supports both.

## Outcome

Memo delivered internally. The production system keeps running unchanged until phase 0 produces the two numbers the decision depends on.
