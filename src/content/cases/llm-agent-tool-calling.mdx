## The client's need

Clinics on the platform took bookings by phone and web forms. The goal was to let people book the way they already talk: a message on WhatsApp or Telegram, in their own words, ending in a real appointment on a real schedule. That means the model cannot just chat. It has to act on live data, across many tenants, without becoming a security hole.

## What I proposed

I own the AI and serverless layer. Messages enter through API Gateway, are acknowledged fast and queued in SQS FIFO, and a second Lambda drives an Amazon Bedrock Agent through the conversation.

## The hard call

> The caller's identity comes from the messaging platform's webhook metadata only. Never from anything the person types or the model generates.

That single rule is the difference between a booking bot and an account-takeover vector, so I specified it as a hard requirement of the runtime.

The second lesson came from a live pilot with practising professionals. I turned what they found into five fixes and shipped them: literal-time handling and overlap detection, city filtering without geodata, correct gendered and plural Spanish, a notice when a session restarts after its 30-minute memory window, and locking the reply language. **Three of the five were prompt changes**, which no unit test can verify. They required structured live conversation testing.

## Outcome

In production, with all five pilot fixes shipped.
