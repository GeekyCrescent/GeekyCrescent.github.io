## The client's need

My own. A large share of my engineering work starts with an AI coding agent, and I wanted to launch it, follow it and approve its actions from my phone, on my own machines, without an API key or a central server. I first built that as a private tool, Jarvis. Factotum is the public rewrite, built so **someone else can clone it and run it with their own subscription without touching the code.**

## What I proposed

A deliberately small core that knows how to do nothing in particular. Everything it can do comes from modules, and each person switches on their own.

Features shipped spec-first since: projects and history, AI-generated titles, file references with Tab completion, visible subagents, background services, several sessions per project and voice dictation.

## The hard call

> Measure first, then delete the feature if the numbers say so.

In Jarvis, per-session git worktrees were designed in from the start. After measuring five real sessions, none had written inside its own worktree, and **8 of 11 approval prompts came from that mechanism**. Removing it was the improvement. Factotum was designed around that lesson.

## Outcome

Open source and in daily use: it is how a good part of my engineering work now gets started. **1,607 automated tests**, 16 architecture decision records. Jarvis, the private predecessor, reached 1,069 unit and 597 end-to-end tests.
