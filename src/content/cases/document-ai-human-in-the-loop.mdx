## The client's need

The legal team of a federal agency tracked cases in personal spreadsheets. Cases were lost because deadlines passed without a response, and hearings were missed. In this domain, one day late can mean the right to respond is gone for good.

## What I proposed

I took it from a client meeting to a **v2.0 requirements document validated by a legal specialist**. Every requirement carries an evidence mark (said by the client, confirmed by the specialist, verified against a primary source, inferred, assumed, open or withdrawn), and numbers are never reused.

Delivery is cut by legal consequence, not technical convenience:

I also produced **three architecture variants** trading data residency against capability (strict residency on GCP, partial on AWS in-country, relaxed on AWS US), three ADRs and 15 numbered technical decisions, plus machine-readable specs so the build can be driven against explicit acceptance criteria.

## The hard call

> Wave 1A uses no AI at all. It is not a bootstrap phase; it is the system's permanent degraded path.

The legal team stops missing hearings while reading quality is still being tuned, and when the model is unavailable, which it will be, the work goes on.

The specialist also corrected the product framing: the lawyers know exactly what to do with a document. What they cannot do is read all of them in time. So the system absorbs volume instead of replacing judgement, and every requirement that looked like replacing a lawyer was withdrawn.

## Outcome

Requirements validated, architecture and specs ready to start Wave 1A. What the specialist did not validate (volume, recovery targets, retention, hardware) stays marked as an assumption. Confirming the domain model does not confirm the sizing.
